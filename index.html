National Hospitalised Injury Trends in Australia Data Visualisation

Chaeyoon Hyun, 105330980 | Adonias Pedro, 104463681

COS30045, Data Visualisation

The line chart shows how many male and female drivers between the ages 40 and 64 were injured in car accidents in Australia from 2011 through 2020. Users of the chart can compare male and female drivers' injuries over time and see when the number of car related injuries changed such as increased, decreased, or remained unchanged in terms of hospitalisation rates. With the information presented interactively with tooltips and graphic comparisons, the statistics about vehicle related injuries become clearer to all members of society.
From 2011 to 2019, Australia saw a gradual increase in both male and female values, with male figures rising from 2.35 to 2.95 and female figures from 35 to 46, before a slight dip in 2020 and a partial recovery in 2021. Notably, female values consistently remained significantly higher than male values across all years, maintaining a gap of roughly 30 to 40 units.
This bar chart compares the number of hospitalised injuries among male and female pedal cyclists aged 40–64 in Australia between 2011 and 2021. By displaying the yearly injury counts side by side and providing interactive tooltips, the visualisation allows users to easily compare gender differences and observe how pedal cyclist hospitalisations have changed over time.
